Case Study

Learning an Operation From the Inside

SupplyHouse.com · Atlanta Fulfillment Center · Summer 2026

My internship happened on a screen. Forty supplier brands, 9,245 SKUs, a forecast queue in RELEX, and a daily judgment call about which numbers had actually moved and which were just noise. Then SupplyHouse flew me to Atlanta for a facility automation decision I had no background in.

I was put on a cross-functional team designing the layout for a new distribution facility built around robotics from the start, rather than automation retrofitted later into a building that was never laid out for it. I knew less about how a warehouse floor works than anyone else in that room. So I contributed what I had — the demand planning perspective — and I built the deck. A teammate with the operations background presented it.

My team didn't win. The proposal that did wasn't the most technically optimized. It was the one that made room for the people who would spend a full shift in the building, down to a Starbucks on site.

At first that sounded soft to me. The operations leaders in that room didn't hear it that way, and they were right: a facility is a throughput system, and it is also somewhere people work eight hours a day. The team that designed for both won the room.

Two things came back with me. The questions I assumed were too basic to ask turned out to be worth asking — the uninformed view had value partly because it was uninformed, and saying plainly what I was observing moved the conversation further than staying quiet would have. And the numbers stopped being abstract. I knew what building they landed in, and who was picking the SKU when my forecast was wrong.

What this is not

I'm not claiming I shaped the decision. The robotics vendor, the contract status, and the facility specifics were not public, so there is no outcome to report here. What I have is the experience of being dropped into an operation I didn't understand and making myself useful inside it — plus a demand planner's sense of how forecast accuracy carries into slotting, robotics utilization, and fulfillment capacity.
